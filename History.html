A Sacred Heritage

Where faith, nature and tradition meet

Rajrappa is home to Maa Chhinnamasta, one of the most revered manifestations of Shakti worship in Jharkhand. The temple stands at the confluence of the Damodar and Bhairavi (Bhera) rivers and has remained an important pilgrimage destination for generations.

“An ancient and powerful centre of Shakti at the confluence of the Damodar and Bhairavi rivers.”

— Based on Jharkhand Government tourism descriptions
The Temple & The Goddess

Maa Chhinnamastika

The main shrine at Rajrappa is dedicated to Maa Chhinnamastika. The old temple is associated with a distinctive form of the Goddess: a headless divine image placed over Kamdev and Rati on a lotus bed.

The Rajrappa temple is described by Jharkhand's official tourism sources as an ancient centre of Shakti, with references to the temple and sacred tradition in the Vedas and Puranas.

The temple complex has also developed into a wider sacred space, with shrines associated with several other Hindu deities.

Sacred Geography

Where Two Rivers Meet

Rajrappa's spiritual identity is inseparable from its landscape. The temple is situated at the confluence of the Bhairavi, also known as Bhera, and the Damodar River.


The meeting of the rivers creates a distinctive natural setting around the temple and has contributed to Rajrappa's importance as a place of pilgrimage and devotion.

🌊 The Confluence

The Damodar and Bhairavi rivers meet at Rajrappa, creating one of the defining geographical features of the temple complex.

Architecture & Sacred Space

A Temple Complex Rich in Tradition

Rajrappa is more than a single shrine. Its architectural character, associated temples and natural surroundings form a larger pilgrimage landscape.

Temple Architecture

Jharkhand Tourism notes that the architecture of Rajrappa bears resemblance to the famous Kamakhya Temple and reflects the temple's distinctive sacred character.

Many Shrines

The temple complex includes shrines connected with deities such as Lord Shiva, the Sun God and Goddess Kali.

A Living Tradition

Devotees continue to visit Rajrappa throughout the year, with especially large gatherings on full-moon and new-moon nights and during major religious occasions.

A Continuing Legacy

Rajrappa Through Time

Ancient Sacred Tradition

Growth of the Temple Complex

The main shrine became part of a larger temple complex containing additional shrines and sacred spaces.

A Major Pilgrimage Destination

Rajrappa attracted devotees from Jharkhand and neighbouring regions, with important gatherings around religious occasions.

Rajrappa in Modern Jharkhand

Today the temple remains a prominent religious and tourism destination in Ramgarh district and continues to welcome pilgrims throughout the year.
